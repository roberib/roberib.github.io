When an iPhone says it is out of storage, the first thing most of us look at
is photos. But the culprit is usually video: a single minute shot in 4K at
60 fps takes about 400 MB, according to the iPhone itself in
**Settings › Camera › Record Video**. A three-minute concert clip outweighs
hundreds of photos.

The good news is that deleting a handful of videos gets you gigabytes back.
Here is how to find them, delete them for real, and keep it from happening
again.

## 1. See how much space Photos takes

Go to **Settings › General › iPhone Storage**. The bar at the top shows what
is using space, and below it your apps are sorted by size. If **Photos** is
near the top, read on. Tap **Show All** under the recommendations: sometimes
iOS already suggests what to review.

## 2. Find the videos

In the **Photos** app, go to **Collections › Media Types › Videos**. That is
every video, with no photos in between. Two places that tend to hide
gigabytes:

- **Screen Recordings**: it is easy to forget how long they run.
- **Slo-mo**: more frames per second means bigger files.

Photos does not sort by size, but the duration on each thumbnail is a good
hint: at the same quality, longer means heavier. To see a video's exact size,
open it and swipe up.

## 3. Delete them… and empty Recently Deleted

Deleting a video does not free any space yet: it goes to the **Recently
Deleted** album, where iOS keeps it for 30 days in case you change your mind.
To get the space back now, in Photos go to **Collections › Recently Deleted**
(under Utilities), unlock it with Face ID, tap **Select**, and delete what you
do not want.

<p class="note">Careful if you use iCloud Photos: deleting a video on your
iPhone also deletes it on your other devices and in iCloud. If you want to
keep it, copy it to a computer or a drive first.</p>

If you have room in iCloud and would rather not delete anything, turn on
**Settings › Photos › Optimize iPhone Storage**: the iPhone keeps smaller
versions and leaves the originals in iCloud.

## 4. Keep it from happening again

- In **Settings › Camera › Record Video**, pick 1080p at 30 fps for everyday
  clips. The iPhone itself estimates about 60 MB a minute, against ~400 MB
  for 4K at 60 fps. Save 4K for what really deserves it.
- In **Settings › Camera › Formats**, keep **High Efficiency**: the same video
  takes noticeably less space.
