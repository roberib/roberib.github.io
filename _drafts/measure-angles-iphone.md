Hanging a picture straight, checking that a shelf does not lean, or finding
out whether a corner is 90° before you cut a baseboard. Your iPhone covers
most of that: it already has a level, and what the level cannot see has a
fix.

## 1. The level it already has: Measure

1. Open the **Measure** app (it is in the Utilities folder).
2. Tap **Level** and hold your iPhone against the object, a picture frame for
   example.
3. To **make it level**, rotate the object and the iPhone until you see
   green.
4. To **match a slope**, tap the screen while the iPhone rests on the first
   object: that captures its slope. Hold it against the second one and rotate
   both until the screen turns green. Tap again to reset.

Apple warns that the measurements are approximate. For a picture or a shelf,
that is plenty.

## 2. What a level cannot measure

A level measures against gravity: it tells you how far something is from
horizontal. That is why it cannot see the corner between two walls: both are
vertical, so to a level they look the same, whether they meet at 90° or 93°.
The same goes for a door frame, or the side of a cabinet against a wall.

Without any app, the old trick is **3-4-5**: from the corner, mark 3 ft on
one wall and 4 ft on the other. If the distance between the marks is exactly
5 ft, the corner is square. More than that and it is open; less and it is
closed. It tells you whether it is square, not how many degrees it is.

## 3. With Diedro: the corner, on any iPhone

[Diedro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6818955422) is an app I made for this.
Its **Angle** tab measures a corner from two rests: rest the back of the
iPhone on one face, then on the other, and the turn between the two rests is
the corner. It does not depend on gravity, so it works for two walls, a floor
and a wall, or the bevel of a blade.

You get the interior angle, the exterior, the supplement and the **miter
saw** setting that joins two pieces in that corner.

## 4. On iPhone Duo, the hinge is the protractor

On iPhone Duo, the first foldable iPhone, Diedro reads the angle of the hinge
itself: open the phone against a corner, and the angle between its two halves
is the measurement.

- **Measure**: the interior angle, with its exterior, supplement and miter
  saw setting.
- **Square**: color and a tap tell you when you reach 90°, 45° and the usual
  workshop angles.
- **Target**: pick an angle and be guided to it, how far and which way. Use
  it to copy an angle from one place to another.
- **Two-plane level**: one half rests on the base and the other rises against
  the slope, and you read both at once.
- A **ruler** whose zero is the fold.
